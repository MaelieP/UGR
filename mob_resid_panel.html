The goal of this script is to grasp, quantify and describe residential mobility of the people & households in the panel :

We use multiple definitions of moving in this script the most flexible one being “change of seccion censal” between two years (given that this is not just a recoding of the section). Further down in this script we prefer to use the change of district between two years. We prefer districts because they are less likely to change between years and because it is easier to link this panel to any other database if we use the district level than the seccion censal (less common).

move = 1 if the person’s census section changed between t−1 and t, unless the change is a recoding (same municipality, ≥ 40 % of the origin section’s households making the same transition, and at least 2 households). Changes of municipality are always counted as moves. NA if the person is not observed in t−1 or the section is missing.

move_dist_ext = 1 if the person’s district changed between their previous observation and year t, allowing a one-year gap. If the person is observed in t−1, it equals move_dist(districts are assumed never recoded) : a change of district (first 7 digits of the census section code) that is not a section recoding. If the person is absent in t−1 but observed in t−2, it is the raw change of district between t−2 and t; recodings cannot be excluded in this case. NA if the person is not observed in t−1 or t−2, or if the district is missing. dist_comp indicates which comparison was used (“t-1” or “t-2”).

=> Tolerating for an extra year of interval doesn’t add that much information and complicates year-level interpretation so we tend to prefer the strict definition : moving = changing district between t-1 and t

Some section changes do not correspond to families moving but to sections being recoded (when a very large share of households in a given section censal move to another section censal in the same municipality) => if over 40% of hh move from the same to the same censal section, we consider to be a recoding

raw_sec = % of moves without taking out the recodages

move = % of moves once the recodages taken away (seccion censal)

move_dist = % of moves as in district level changes

mun / prov = % of change of munipality / province

origin / dest same_group (no newcomers) with_others (with newcomers)
whole (entire household, including one-person households) the household moves as it is the household moves in with others
part (part of the household leaves) leaving the household (leaving the parental home, separation…) leaving to live with someone

Key findings :

  • Every year, between 4 and 6% of people move (as in change seccion censal) every year /!\ maybe our criterion of seccion recoding is too strict <=> we should consider more moves as recoding. Around 3% change district, 2% change municipality and only 0,7% change province.

  • Between 2 and 3% of households change district , around 2% change municipality and 0,7% province.

  • More than 800.000 movers in total in the panel (more if we consider seccion censal level moving)

  • 53% of the moves (district level) are entire households moving whilst 32% of them leave their previous household to live alone (students or separation). 8,6% of moves are people (total households) moving into another household. 4% are people leaving a previous household to integrate a new one

  • ~ 3,5% of people aged up to 24 yrs old have moved in the year. This rate goes up to 7% of 24-34 yrs old.

  • Comparing with INE : the Panel catches ~ 70% of the moves registrated by INE => certainly underestimated because the INE uses Padrón info whilst Panel follows people within a sample. Moreover, the most residentially mobile people are less likely to remain within the panel. For example, 9.6% of the persons who entered the panel before 2023 are missing for at least one year before reappearing. Among those who missed one year, 24.2% live in a different municipality when they reappear, against 3.2% among persons observed in two consecutive years, this gap suggests that the most mobile people are the ones the panel follows least well.
